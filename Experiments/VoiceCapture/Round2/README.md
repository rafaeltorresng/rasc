# Rasc Voice Capture study · November 24, 2026

**Go for a standalone study. No-Go for production or default automatic Keep.** Use Fluid Speech Pro and Parakeet Ultra as the two native finalists, with Apple SpeechTranscriber as the control. Ultra had the lowest held-out Brazilian Portuguese WER and did better than Fluid on the three synthetic mixed clips. Fluid had a slight edge on held-out English and led on the Portuguese screening split. Neither established reliable code-switching on spontaneous human thoughts. Both incurred model preparation delays of 20–37 seconds in measured benchmark runs. Choosing one today would overstate the evidence.

## What you can use now

Open **Rasc Voice Study**, start a recording, grant microphone access, then start again. Wait for **Listening**, speak, and finish. The default opens editable review with a **Show Raw** control and **Keep**. A saved thought can be opened or undone. **Study Options** lets you switch engines, select the Apple locale, prepare a model, assign a global shortcut, choose toggle or hold, and compare review with both automatic completion modes. The two external models ship inside this local study build. Apple may need a one-time asset download for the selected language. [Study source](Study/) · [Validation output](results/study-validation.txt)

The recording panel uses Rasc’s native surface and theme code, a real input-level meter, and a copper Kept receipt. It uses a non-key panel on the pointer’s display. Menus and the study window provide explicit cancellation and a path back to the last receipt. Permission and review can bring up the study window. In automatic mode, success keeps focus where it was by design. This configuration still needs physical verification in fullscreen, Spaces, Stage Manager, multiple displays, and with assistive technologies. The study uses batch recognition after Finish for all three options. Streaming findings below come from separate probes. [Interface](Study/VoiceStudy.swift) · [Session](Study/StudySession.swift)

The SQLite file belongs to the study container. The capture UUID doubles as the note ID for idempotent writes. The repository inserts an ordinary Kept note in one transaction and reports success after commit. The existing study scratch belongs to a different record and table. The automated signed-fixture test exercised Apple recognition, editable review, one successful insertion, preserved scratch and Undo against a temporary database. Mic permission was checked separately. A repository failure leaves the text available to retry. Module preparation and recognition results are scoped to the capture, and normal quit asks the user to resolve a pending capture. A crash or force quit can lose memory-only audio. ## What was measured

On the target **Apple M3 Pro with 18 GB**, Mac15,6, macOS 26.5.1 build 25F80, Xcode 26.2 and Swift 6.2.3. The first-round Rasc baseline and 538 passing shared-scheme tests are historical evidence at `250f1c1c82534bc69daad056fced69c083d298d7`. This round used the same Rasc source for the lab and verified that the remote main ref still pointed to that commit. No shipping source, settings, entitlements, feed or release files changed. [Host and study entitlements](results/host-and-study-entitlements.txt) · [Baseline test output](../results/rasc-baseline.txt)

- **Screen** used 32 Brazilian Portuguese human conversation clips from Tarsila-ASR, 32 clean English human read clips, and 23 matched synthetic files including three controls. The second pair of index blocks in each public dataset forms the separate **held** split, another 32 PT and 32 EN clips. The PT samples come from the NURC-SP conversation source. Index separation is verified. Speaker disjointness and training overlap are unknown. Round 1 synthetic examples are for development only. Two further hand-authored synthetic checks target “Supabase” and a timeout change. [Recording hashes and offsets](results/human-manifest-no-text.json) · [Tarsila Interspeech paper](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2026/leal26_interspeech.html) · [Tarsila source](https://github.com/nilc-nlp/tarsila-asr) · [LibriSpeech](https://www.openslr.org/12)
- Shared mono PCM at 16 kHz. No vocabulary hints, model-specific answer injection, Refine, translation prompts, or LLM cleanup. Apple used the appropriate fixed language for each monolingual group and pt-BR for Portuguese-led mixed fixtures. Fluid, Ultra, and MLX candidates used default language handling. Nemotron was tested in auto and then on a few separate pt-BR probes. Whisper used the same fixed-locale policy as Round 1. Fluid ran its verbatim path. These are deployable configuration comparisons rather than a claim that prompting or each runtime is equivalent. [Batch runner](run-bench.py) · [Model lock](results/model-lock.json)
- WER and CER are pooled edit counts per scenario. Case, spacing and punctuation are normalized for WER, with accents retained. Formatting such as `180` versus written-out numbers can inflate WER even when the meaning is correct. For no speech, the result is reported directly. The scorer includes first/last token matches, duration groups, noise, proper nouns, URLs, uncertainty and other tags. The examples below were reviewed for meaning separately. Natural PT–EN switching, moderate real background noise, and freeform short and long thoughts still need matched human speech. ### Errors that matter in a thought

| Error type | Observed examples on controlled test speech |
|---|---|
| Missing negation or end of thought | Nemotron heard the zero-guard “Don’t deploy yet” as “Deplo,” losing both ends. Apple, Fluid and Ultra kept both ends in that fixture. The Cohere Swift MLX smoke rendered “Não apaga isso” as “Now apaga isso” in automatic mode. An explicit Portuguese hint restored it in the one matched probe. |
| Wrong final quantity | Nemotron omitted the self-correction “não, dois” and saved a request for three coffees instead of two. Fluid and Ultra retained the correction. Punctuation placed a full stop after “não,” so even the retained words may require clarification. |
| Broken technical term or uncertainty | Nemotron preserved “race condition no worker” and “não sei” in the main mixed fixture. Fluid and Ultra recognized all key terms and added “o” inside “não o sei.” Fixed-PT Apple rendered “Race condiam … Orquê” and damaged the uncertainty clause. Qwen 1.7B rendered “não ousei” in that same clause. |
| Proper nouns and identifiers | “Supabase” became “super base” on Apple and “supa base” on Fluid and Ultra. In the timeout supplement, the numbers 180 and 80 and the SDK survived on both portable engines. All three mangled the term “timeout.” The numeric formats differ without changing the values. |
| Person, place and date | On the Lívia / João Siqueira / Fortaleza clip, Apple, Fluid and Ultra preserved the people and the location relation. Granite, Nemotron and Whisper-v3 used “e Fortaleza.” A small word altered the relationship. |
| Unsupported speech | Apple and Nemotron returned empty for all three no-speech files. Fluid and Ultra returned empty for digital silence and colored noise and refused the click-only clip below their one-second minimum. Granite emitted punctuation on silence. Qwen 0.6B emitted Cyrillic text on the click. Whisper Large-v3 and Round 1 Turbo emitted words on all three controls. |
| Translation and readability | No system in the main benchmark was asked to translate. The Cohere mixed rendering and isolated foreign-script insertion are recognition errors. Whole-utterance unwanted translation rates, subjective readability, and actual time to correct require held-out human scoring. |

Treat a meaning change as critical if it can change the action, the degree of certainty, the referenced entity or amount, or whether the user expressed it at all. Record the missing, added or substituted span and whether it appears at a boundary. A formatting variant counts as a correction only when a participant needs to edit it for intended use. Do not let raw WER or model confidence decide whether automatic Keep is safe. Short means at most six seconds of audio. Native batch timings start after WAV ingest and include per-file preparation and decode. MLX preparation is separate and decode includes reading the WAV. Nemotron's drain begins **after** chunks are fed and excludes both setup and time spent processing during capture. No first nonempty partial was recorded in the three paced rows. Do not treat any of these as microphone-to-Keep latency. Include sample-count and maximum columns when interpreting p95, especially when a first file paid cold load. These are same-Mac process-start samples, not a controlled cache-cleared or post-reboot distribution. A Fluid smoke run prepared in 0.43 s with warm system caches. First activation after idle remains an important unresolved difference. Mac memory counters, MLX allocator usage, and model storage measure different things. No comparable GPU/ANE, energy, idle memory, disk-cache state, or memory-constrained Mac measurements were collected. [Per-clip human timings](results/human-timings.jsonl) · [Process resource samples](results/resource-samples.txt) · [Exact sizes](results/model-lock.json) · [Nemotron study](Nemotron/Probe.swift)

The practical frontier has three distinct choices. **Apple** is the best zero-added-weights and first-use control. **Ultra and Fluid** buy better Portuguese WER on these slices and preserve more technical detail in the key example, at the cost of native dependencies, 0.6 GB each, and a potentially long cold preparation. Neither clearly dominates the other. **Nemotron** remains a follow-up for streaming because end-of-speech drain is attractive and the key mixed sentence held up, but edge loss and self-correction loss exclude it from personal-test automatic Keep. Granite is the fastest measured warm batch and the strongest English read result. Its PT held WER and process footprint keep it off the native shortlist. Qwen-1.7B leads neither the live-study quality trade-off nor latency and footprint. PT and mixed quality outweighed Granite’s 15 ms warm-decode advantage.

## Interaction recommendation

Make **toggle** the primary study interaction and default the study to **Review before Keep**. Toggle sustains natural pauses and longer thoughts with less physical effort. Hold is worth a controlled comparison for short bursts and accidental activation, especially with physical modifier rollover and release timing. The current mapping consumes down/up, debounces using key state, finishes on release for hold, and toggles on key-up. Keep using the existing global shortcut library and measure physical behavior before adding any input monitoring. A first tap is enough for explicit acknowledgment in a future shipping toggle if the physical trial supports moving activation to key-down.

The compact panel shows **Starting microphone**, **Listening**, and **Transcribing** with a real meter when listening. During the study, require useful consent before recording and explicit finish, with no silence-based termination. The legacy mic probe reached the first input buffer at repeat median 187 ms and first 350 ms. The study logs first input and status-ordering request separately, and no actual screen-composite timestamp was measured. Test Bluetooth, routing and release separately from file trims. Use a short deferred post-roll only if physical trials show a boundary benefit and the panel visibly indicates it is still receiving audio. [First-round mic timings](../results/microphone.jsonl) · [Study protocol](TEST-PROTOCOL.md)

If measured fidelity and product value warrant automatic Keep, prefer a short **Kept** receipt with **Open** and **Undo** over a forced review or a receipt with no recovery affordance. Handle silence, cancellation, interrupted capture, decode errors and write failures explicitly. Keep the existing inactive draft separate. Use normal casing and punctuation from the engine and preserve the recognized words. Refine remains a separate deliberate action. Do not condition review on unsupported confidence estimates. The stable scratch-to-voice comparison still requires measuring activation through *correct, saved thought and resumed work* on short and longer thoughts. Typing will likely remain the quickest option for small edits, precision strings, and shared rooms. A dedicated voice shortcut has the clearest possible advantage on longer unstructured thoughts, when hands are occupied, and when typing is difficult. These are product hypotheses; no measured typing versus Apple Dictation versus global-voice outcome yet.

## Privacy and distribution

The sandboxed study targets Apple Silicon on macOS 26. Rasc’s deployment target is macOS 14. Supporting portable weights on macOS 14 or 15 would require separate builds and verification of the full app path, including assets and sandbox behavior. There is no evidence yet that maintaining an older native path would bring enough additional value.

The study binary is Hardened Runtime and App Sandbox signed with only the audio-input entitlement in addition to sandbox. Microphone access is contextual, limited to explicit sessions. Core ML assets are bundled from pinned byte-checked inputs and the code accepts a local model path. Python MLX used locally staged folders with Hugging Face and Transformers offline flags. Apple asset preparation is OS-managed and can download assets. No app code sends speech, note content or diagnostics to a service. The study opens the microphone only to capture input, stops it on Finish/cancel, and keeps audio in memory unless you choose **Save Evaluation Audio**. Raw text is shown separately during review. The default persisted thought is the user’s final text only. Saved study WAVs belong to the study container and are explicitly removable. A nested global network-denial attempt exited before the prototype ran, so physical Wi-Fi-off/restart inference and system-service observations are manual gates. [Entitlements](results/host-and-study-entitlements.txt) · [Audio handling](Study/Audio.swift)

**Genuine Sparkle update reproduced microphone permission loss.** A local-only Sparkle 2.9.4 update downloaded an Ed25519-signed ZIP, installed build 2 at the same path, and relaunched it. Build 1 had a user-granted status of 3 (authorized). Build 2 reported 0 (not determined) on first launch despite the same bundle identifier and install path. Its ad-hoc designated requirement was a different CDHash. The same build 1 retained authorization when relocated and when reinstalled over the same path with the original CDHash. The fixture never opened the microphone. It used the release-style hardened sandbox, downloader and installer XPCs, and a Sparkle library-load CDHash requirement. This isolates a real update path and signer difference on this Mac. Developer ID continuity, an explicit deny/allow round-trip after update, and model compatibility across an actual full study upgrade remain unverified. Prove continuity with a stable signing identity or prove that contextual reauthorization is reliable and acceptable before shipping. ## Gates for the next decision

1. **Quality.** Freeze development settings, then evaluate at least 50 genuinely spontaneous held-out thoughts each in PT-BR, English and mixed speech across multiple consenting speakers. Include quiet and noisy clips, 20 short boundary trials per language, and separately count every high-consequence mistake. Proposed targets before automatic Keep are zero observed action-changing critical errors in that held-out set, ≥99% first/last lexical token preservation on physical shortcut cases and zero notes from 100 silent, interrupted or cancelled trials without valid completion. Report sample sizes and uncertainty. Keep review if the product is still useful with it.
2. **Correct thought and return.** Counterbalance typing, Apple Dictation and voice in short and long tasks. Measure from activation until the correct thought is durably saved and the prior app is ready to use. Include errors, number of corrective actions and focus changes. Require a meaningful benefit for longer captures or accessibility (study target at least 20% median time saved for one of those populations, with no increase in critical errors). Measure the costs for short thoughts even if typing wins.
3. **Performance.** On the target Mac and a lower-memory Apple Silicon Mac, measure p50/p95 status display, first mic buffer, first partial for streaming only, final text, commit, and total after fresh launch, warmed short loop and at least 10 minutes idle. Aim for ≤100 ms acknowledgment p95, ≤300 ms mic readiness p95 when permission is already granted, and ≤1 s prepared short-thought finalization plus commit p95. Permit a slower finalist if correction effort and the time to return are lower. Treat 20+ second model loads as a separate product problem. Set an idle-memory budget after profiling with Instruments or equivalent. Keep the mic inactive while idle and release model resources on a documented bound.
4. **Correctness.** Under repeated key events, flaky mic routing, sleep, late decode, cancelled initialization and injected DB failure, assert no scratch modifications, duplicates or premature Kept receipt. Confirm deterministic retry or discard and study app lifecycle cleanup. Test focus, physical keys, headphones, multiple displays, accessibility and interruption in a sandboxed optimized build.
5. **Release feasibility.** Validate network-disconnected inference after clean setup and after update, and prove pinned-snapshot integrity, model license attribution, separately review native dependencies and download surfaces, and demonstrate acceptable permission continuity with production-equivalent signing. Decide the macOS and hardware boundary only then. If no portable model passes the combined quality and setup gates, rerun the value task with Apple alone before extending availability.
